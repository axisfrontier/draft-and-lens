## Key research findings from tonight's detection design discussion

**The core mathematical constraint:** false positives and false negatives trade off against each other in any detection system — this is true across virus scanning, fraud detection, hallucination detection, and fact-checking research. You cannot minimize both simultaneously with a single threshold. This isn't a D&L engineering gap; it's a property of the problem itself, confirmed across multiple independent domains of research.

**Industry-confirmed scope boundary (important, likely worth stating publicly/in marketing eventually):** Every serious competitor (Novarrium, Inkfluence AI, and independent reviewers) draws the same line D&L's detection is now being built to respect: AI can reliably check *mechanical* continuity (names, dates, physical descriptions, stated facts). AI cannot reliably judge *interpretive* continuity (pacing consistency, deliberate unreliable narration, intentional misdirection) — this is explicitly stated as needing a human editor/beta reader, industry-wide. This is worth remembering as a talking point later: D&L isn't limited here compared to competitors — nobody has solved this, and honestly saying so is itself a trust signal ("we tell you what we can't check" fits the whole brand).

**Best-known architecture pattern (Novarrium's "Logic-Locking"):** extraction → structured fact storage → comparison-based detection → severity classification → immutable/locked facts for the highest-stakes claims. This matches what's already been built tonight (extraction, locks, and now severity-tiered detection) — D&L is following the same shape as the most sophisticated competitor found, independently arrived at rather than copied.

**Failure-mode vocabulary worth knowing, if this ever needs deeper technical discussion:**
- "Granularity mismatch" — different levels of specificity for the same fact type causing false negatives (most common failure type in the research reviewed).
- "Parametric shortcut" — a model filling a gap from its own general knowledge rather than the actual source text, passing a check that should fail. Same failure family as the "lyricism" and "lightbulb/storm" bugs found earlier tonight.
- Confident-sounding false claims are harder to catch than uncertain ones, across every study reviewed — worth remembering if a future false-positive bug report comes in and looks "too plausible to be wrong."

## Known constraints — built as designed, less sensitive than the spec sounds

*(Logged 2026-08-20. These are not bugs and no fix is planned. They are recorded so that if a writer ever reports one, it is recognised as a known boundary rather than diagnosed from scratch — and so the copy never promises more than the feature does.)*

**Continuity checks see extracted FACTS, not appearances.** §5.7 describes a state-lock violation as the locked character appearing "as a live participant in present-tense narration" at a later point. What the ledger can actually see is narrower: a violation is raised only where the later chapter *asserts something extractable* about that character — a name, a physical detail, an age or date, a relationship — **in the book's own voice**. So a character can be on the page for an entire chapter, spoken to, acting, central to the scene, and stay invisible to the check if nothing about her is asserted in narration.

Observed directly on 2026-08-19 in the live state-lock test. Chapter 2 had the locked character present throughout and raised nothing — correctly, because every fact extracted about her came back `register = dialogue`, and §5.2 rightly excludes those: a character's claim about someone is not the book putting them on the page. Chapter 3 raised the violation, and only because it stated physical facts about her in narration.

Two reasons this stays as it is rather than being "fixed":
- The gate is behaving exactly as written. Loosening it means either treating dialogue as the book's own voice (which breaks §5.2 and would flag every book where someone is talked about after they die — i.e. most of them) or detecting presence without extraction, which is a different feature with a different cost and risk profile.
- It fails in the safe direction. The failure mode is a quiet miss, not a false accusation, and §5.4 is explicit that a wrong firm flag is the expensive error.

**What this means for copy, and it matters:** a death lock that stays silent must never be read as "the manuscript is clean". It means nothing was found, which is not the same thing. This is the same honesty boundary as the industry-wide mechanical/interpretive line above, and it belongs in the same "we tell you what we can't check" register — the tool reports what it saw, and says plainly that its sight is partial.

**The two gates previously logged here as "starved of input" both fired on 2026-08-20 — this entry is superseded and kept only so the earlier reading is not mistaken for current.** What was actually true:

A chapter with no such centres will still produce nothing, which is the honest answer rather than a failure.

The remaining limit is real but narrower than it looked — a map is only produced at 4,000 words or more, and the tester cap rejects anything above 4,000, so today exactly one submission length can produce one. That is the `FREE_WORD_LIMIT` / `TESTER_WORD_CAP` decision, and it is now the only thing standing in the way.
