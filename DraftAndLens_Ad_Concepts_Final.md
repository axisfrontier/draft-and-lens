# Draft & Lens — Advertising Concepts

Final shortlist from creative exploration session, 2026-08-15. Each concept includes final copy, design direction, and a ready-to-use prompt for external image-generation tools (Midjourney, DALL-E, etc.).

**Brief across all concepts:** target audience is serious/literary writers, skeptical of AI. Lead with craft/editorial expertise — AI is secondary, never the headline. No generic AI iconography (no glowing brains, no laptop-with-sparkles, no robot hands). Analogue, editorial, paper-and-type aesthetic throughout — the opposite of sleek-tech-gradient visual language. Never claim D&L "doesn't write" — it gives assistive suggestions like a real editor, but never rewrites a sentence for the writer. Keep that distinction precise in any copy variations.

---

## 1. "The book that knows"

**Copy:**
> This book was edited by an AI. It never once wrote a sentence for me. It just kept telling me which ones weren't earning their place — until I fixed them myself.

**Design direction:** A single real book, closed, plain unbranded jacket, resting upright against a plain wall or on a bare shelf. Clinical, quiet lighting — Rolls-Royce ad register: understated, almost boring on purpose, so the copy carries the tension. Small serif italic copy in the lower third or corner. No laptop, no screen, no AI iconography anywhere in frame.

**Image-gen prompt:**
```
A full print advertisement composition, photographed in the style of a 1960s minimalist ad (Rolls-Royce / Volkswagen Think Small era). A single closed hardcover book with a plain, unbranded, matte cream jacket, resting upright and slightly angled against a bare off-white wall on a plain wooden shelf edge. Soft directional natural window light from the left, minimal shadow. The book sits in the upper two-thirds of the frame with generous negative space around it. In the lower-left third of the composition, small italic serif typography (like Georgia or a classic book-serif, dark warm grey, not pure black) reads: "This book was edited by an AI. It never once wrote a sentence for me. It just kept telling me which ones weren't earning their place — until I fixed them myself." set in three or four short left-aligned lines with generous line spacing. In the bottom-right corner, a small serif wordmark reads "draft&lens" in the same dark warm grey, much smaller than the body copy. Overall palette: warm cream, soft grey, warm white. Portrait aspect ratio, editorial print-ad layout, quiet and restrained, high production value. Photographic realism for the book, clean typesetting for the text — the whole image should read as a finished, ready-to-publish advertisement, not a product photo with no copy.
```

## 2. "Does she… or doesn't she?"

**Copy:**
> Does it need work… or does she just think it does?
>
> *Most writers can't tell the difference. A reading can.*

**Design direction:** Text-only. No image, or as close to none as possible. Generous white/cream negative space matching D&L's own in-product background tone. Centred or left-aligned serif typography, editorial magazine-page feel. The absence of imagery is deliberate — a face or illustration would wrongly imply the ad is about appearance rather than the writer's private self-doubt.

**Image-gen prompt:**
```
A full print advertisement composition: pure typography on a warm cream, subtly paper-textured background — no photographic imagery, no icons, no illustrations. Large elegant serif headline text (dark warm charcoal, not pure black) centred in the upper half of the frame, reading: "Does it need work… or does she just think it does?" Below it, smaller italic serif subtext reading: "Most writers can't tell the difference. A reading can." In the bottom-right corner, a small serif wordmark reads "draft&lens", noticeably smaller than the headline. Generous white space and margins throughout, magazine editorial page style from 1960s-1970s advertising (think Clairol "Does she… or doesn't she?" campaign era). Subtle visible paper grain texture on the background. Portrait aspect ratio. The composition should read as a finished, ready-to-run print advertisement with headline, subtext, and small logo mark all present and properly weighted against each other.
```

## 3. "We know" (self-deprecating honesty, sharpened with defiant comparison)

**Copy (original):**
> Every AI writing tool claims it understands your book. Nobody's proven it yet.
>
> *We show our reasoning instead of asking you to trust it.*

**Copy (variant, same structural logic as revised concept 9 below — concede the doubt, answer with evidence not superiority):**
> Most AI writing tools ask you to trust that they understood your book. That's a reasonable thing to doubt.
>
> *So we don't ask. We show you exactly what we read it against, and why.*

**Design direction:** Either a plain, empty text-editor window with a blinking cursor and nothing written (signals "screen-based tool" honestly without tech-gradient styling), or typeset copy only on a stark plain background — no imagery at all. Deadpan, confident tone — borrows the Porsche "Nobody's Perfect" / Citroën "Faster than Ferrari" technique: name the category's shared weakness before the reader can raise it themselves, then position D&L as the one addressing it honestly rather than avoiding the comparison entirely.

**Image-gen prompt:**
```
A full print advertisement composition. Upper two-thirds of the frame: a minimalist screenshot-style mockup of a plain, empty text editor window on a neutral light grey background — a simple rectangular window frame, a thin cursor blinking on an otherwise blank page, no toolbar clutter, no branding, extremely spare and flat, no gradients or glow effects. Below the window, in the lower third of the composition, left-aligned sans-serif copy in dark charcoal reads: "Every AI writing tool claims it understands your book. Nobody's proven it yet." on its own line, then after a visible line break: "We show our reasoning instead of asking you to trust it." In the bottom-right corner, a small wordmark reads "draft&lens", clearly smaller than the body copy. Muted matte colour palette — soft greys and off-white throughout. Portrait aspect ratio. The composition should read as a finished, confident, deadpan print advertisement.
```

## 4. "How would you know?"

**Copy:**
> A human editor's read hundreds of books like yours. How do you know an AI has?
>
> You don't. So we show our working — what tradition we read this against, what we compared it to, and why. Check it yourself.

**Design direction:** A real manuscript page with visible marginalia — a genuine-looking handwritten note in the margin, an underline beneath a sentence. Should look like evidence of a real, close read, not decoration. Warm paper tone, natural desk lighting.

**Image-gen prompt:**
```
A full print advertisement composition. A close-up photograph of a printed manuscript page on warm cream paper, resting on a wooden desk, filling the upper two-thirds of the frame, with a single handwritten pencil annotation in the margin and one sentence in the main text underlined in pencil. Natural soft window light from the side, shallow depth of field with the annotation in sharp focus. In the lower third of the composition, on a clean cream margin below the manuscript, left-aligned serif copy in dark warm grey reads: "A human editor's read hundreds of books like yours. How do you know an AI has?" followed by smaller text: "You don't. So we show our working — what tradition we read this against, what we compared it to, and why. Check it yourself." In the bottom-right corner, a small serif wordmark reads "draft&lens". Warm, literary, tactile atmosphere throughout. Portrait aspect ratio. Photographic realism for the manuscript, clean typesetting for the copy below it — a finished, ready-to-run advertisement, not a bare product photo.
```

## 7. "Coverage" (screenwriter series)

**Copy:**
> Coverage tells you if your script is sellable. It was never built to tell you if it's good.
>
> *Different question. Different reading.*

**Design direction:** A studio coverage form — the industry-standard PASS / CONSIDER / RECOMMEND checkbox format every screenwriter dreads — with one box deliberately left unchecked. Courier typeface, industry-authentic.

```

## 9. "They said" (VW "Prove them wrong" homage)

**Copy (original — flagged as flawed, kept for reference):**
> They said an AI could never read a manuscript the way an editor does.
>
> It still can't rewrite one. That's the part it was never supposed to do.

*Problem with the original: the setup is about reading ability, the payoff is about rewriting — two different claims, so the "twist" doesn't land as a twist, it reads as a non-sequitur.*

**Copy (fixed — setup and payoff now both about the same claim, reading ability, and framed as consistency rather than a jab at human editors):**
> They said an AI could never read a manuscript the way an editor does.
>
> It doesn't. It reads every page the same way, every time — which is more than most editors can promise.

**Design direction:** Text-only, same restrained treatment as concepts 2 and 6 — the rhythm of the copy carries the ad. Borrows Volkswagen's "They said it couldn't be done. It couldn't" structure: state the doubt plainly, then land somewhere more honest and unexpected than a simple rebuttal. The fixed version avoids any friction with human editors — the claim is about consistency, not competence, so it's not a superiority jab.

**Image-gen prompt:**
```
A full print advertisement composition: pure typography on a warm cream, subtly paper-textured background, no photographic imagery. Large serif headline text (dark warm charcoal) centred in the upper half of the frame, reading: "They said an AI could never read a manuscript the way an editor does." Below it, smaller italic serif subtext reading: "It still can't rewrite one. That's the part it was never supposed to do." In the bottom-right corner, a small serif wordmark reads "draft&lens", noticeably smaller than the headline. Generous margins, quiet and confident editorial typesetting, 1960s advertising style (Volkswagen Think Small era). Subtle visible paper grain. Portrait aspect ratio. ## Notes on use

- Prompts are written for photorealistic output; adjust language (e.g. "illustrated in the style of...") if using a tool better suited to illustration than photography.
- Keep the "no AI iconography" rule in every generation — regenerate rather than accept results with glowing brains, robot hands, circuit patterns, or sci-fi tech styling, none of which fit the brand.
- Text-only concepts (2, 3, 6) may render better as actual typeset graphics (Canva, Figma, or similar) rather than through an image-generation model, since most image-gen tools struggle with precise, legible typography at this level of polish.
